# The 3D mesh renderer `M3D`

*2026-11-14, owner's decision: the sector renderer (`W3D`, `docs/3d-subsystem.md`) cannot express Deqq's House II (a town round a house, tall things seen over fences, rooms over rooms), so a renderer without sectors was written next to it. The sector renderer stays for the other demos (Manor, Bug Hunt, the showroom, Deqq's House I). This page is the reference for the new one; the code is `m3d.[ch]` (no SDL, no global state), the test is `m3dtest1.c`.*

> The sector renderer `W3D` (`docs/3d-subsystem.md`) stays: it is a supported feature with its own demos (Manor, Bug Hunt, Showroom, Deqq's House I, Y2K38's server room, the map editor), and nobody plans to remove it (owner, 2026-11-14). Which renderer fits depends on the world: sectors are fast and simple for rooms joined by portals; meshes can put anything anywhere.

## Model

- A world is a set of **objects** (0 .. 16383). An object is a triangle mesh with a position, a heading (yaw about the z axis), a light level (0 .. 255) and flags. The mesh is in the object's own coordinates; `M3D OBJPOS o,x,y,z[,yaw]` moves and turns it (a door is an object that turns about its hinge).
- **Things** (0 .. 1023) are billboards: a picture of a given width and height that always faces the viewer, with its bottom at `z`.
- Coordinates: x east, y north, z up; angles in degrees, 0 = +x, 90 = +y; to the right of the view direction is clockwise. A triangle is **counterclockwise seen from its front** (the normal `(b-a) x (c-a)` points to the viewer); one-sided objects do not draw or collide with the back. `M3D OBJECT o,16` (two sided) draws both sides.
- Pictures are `GFX GRAB` blocks (`M3D TEXTURE n,a$`, as for `W3D`); palette index 0 is see-through where the object is masked (flag 1) or translucent (flag 2) and always in things. A picture number with no picture draws that palette color flat.
- `M3D BOX o,x1,y1,z1,x2,y2,z2,tex[,faces]` adds the faces of a box (picture coordinates 1 texel = 1 unit, anchored to the object, so neighbouring boxes match; `faces` is a bit mask of the sides to build: 1 +x, 2 -x, 4 +y, 8 -y, 16 top, 32 bottom, default 63 = all, so a box can leave out a side that a quad with another picture covers: a house front with a door). `M3D VERTEX o,x,y,z[,u,v]` numbers vertices from 0 per object; `M3D FACE o,a,b,c,tex` and `M3D QUAD o,a,b,c,d,tex` add triangles; without `u,v` the picture coordinates follow the position by the direction of the face.
- Object flags: 1 masked, 2 translucent (mixed half and half, drawn last, far to near), 4 **passable** (no collision and no floor or ceiling), 8 **see-through** (shots, `USE` of walls and sight pass: glass, a railing), 16 two sided.

## Drawing

Every triangle is culled (back face, behind the viewer, off the screen), clipped against the near plane (2 units) and rasterized with a z buffer and perspective-correct picture coordinates; the light level of the object, the lamps (`M3D LAMP n,x,y,z,radius,amount[,dx,dy,dz,angle]`, up to 8, per pixel, brightening only; with a direction and a half angle in degrees the lamp is a **cone**: full on the axis, nothing at the edge, linear in the cosine of the angle to the axis, times the distance falloff; without them it shines all round) and the fog (`M3D FOG d`: dark at distance d) pick a row of the color map (`M3D COLORMAP` builds it from the palette). Order: the sky (`M3D SKY`, a picture around the viewer by angle), opaque and masked objects, things (far to near, depth tested), translucent objects (far to near). `M3D FAR d` skips what lies beyond d. Cost: about 1.6 ms for a test scene at 320 x 150 (`M3D VIEW x,y,w,h,2`).

## Collision, floors, rays

The player is a vertical capsule (`M3D PLAYER radius,height,eye,step`, default 14, 56, 46, 24), `M3D CAMERA x,y,z,yaw[,pitch]` puts the **feet**. `M3D MOVE forward,right` moves in small pieces, pushes the capsule out of the triangles it touches (spheres at the middle heights; everything lower than `step` above the feet is walked over), slides along an axis when blocked, refuses a floor more than `step` higher, refuses a ceiling lower than the player's height, and then lets the feet follow the floor (`M3D("FLOORZ")`, `M3D("FLOORAT",x,y,z)` = the highest surface at or below z, `("CEILAT",x,y,z)` the lowest above). `M3D LIFT h` raises the feet above the floor (a jump: steps up to `step` above the raised feet are taken). `M3D("HIT")`: 1 = something pushed, 2 = no movement at all.
Rays use the same triangles: `M3D("SHOOT",range)` (things and objects, the last shot is in `SHOTKIND` 0 / 1 object / 2 thing, `SHOTOBJ`, `SHOTDIST`, `SHOTX/Y/Z`), `M3D("USE",reach)` (the object in front, whatever its flags), `M3D("SEES",n)` (a thing's line of sight to the eye).

## Zones, walking things, the world document

- **Zones:** `M3D ZONE id,x1,y1,x2,y2,z1,z2[,tag]` names a box of space for the game (a room); `M3D("ZONE",x,y,z)` is the id of the first zone that contains the point (z within z1 - 2 .. z2), -1 if none, `M3D("ZONETAG",x,y,z)` its tag. The renderer does nothing with them. Put the zones of an upper floor and of a chair or table you can stand on *before* the zones below them. `M3D CLEAR` deletes them.
- **`M3D THINGMOVE n,dx,dy`** walks a thing like `M3D MOVE` walks the player (a body of radius 0.4 x width, at most 14, the player's step height; it follows the floor); `M3D("HIT")` tells whether a wall pushed it.
- **`M3D WORLD`** builds the world from the current JSON document (`JSON LOAD` first), replacing objects, things, lamps and zones; pictures and settings stay:
- **The ground: `M3D GROUND`, `M3D GROUNDFLAT`, `M3D TERRAIN`, `M3D("GROUND",x,y)`** (2026-11-20, roadmap item 95): a ground height function that lives in C (`m3d_ground()` in `m3d.c`, on the noise of `noise.c`, `docs/noise.md`) and a statement that fills an object with a grid of it. `M3D GROUND seed,scale,amp,octaves[,base[,gain[,lacunarity]]]` defines `height = base + amp * FBM(seed, x / scale, y / scale, octaves, gain, lacunarity)` (scale > 0; octaves 1 .. 16; base 0, gain 0.5, lacunarity 2 by default). `M3D GROUNDFLAT n,cx,cy,rx,ry,margin` (n = 0 .. 7) flattens the ground towards 0 round a box: the height is multiplied by `1 - w`, where `w` is 1 inside the box (half sizes `rx`, `ry`) and falls smoothly to 0 over `margin` units outside it (`(1-d)^2 (1+2d)`, d the distance in margins); margin 0 removes slot n. **`M3D TERRAIN o,x0,y0,nx,ny,cell,tex[,cells$[,skirt[,uvscale]]]`** adds to object `o` (made with `M3D OBJECT` first) a grid of `nx` x `ny` cells (1 .. 256) of `cell` units from `(x0,y0)`: `(nx+1)(ny+1)` vertices at the ground's height, two triangles a cell, counterclockwise seen from above (the same as `M3D QUAD o,b,b+1,b+nx+2,b+nx+1`). The picture of a cell is `tex` plus the code of its character in `cells$` (one character per cell, row after row from `y0` on, `nx` to a row; empty = `tex` everywhere; strings are bytes, so offsets 0 .. 255); `skirt` > 0 hangs a wall of that depth under the four edges (it hides the cracks between a coarse grid and its finer neighbour, for levels of detail); the picture coordinates are the position times `uvscale` (0.5 by default: 2 units a texel, absolute, so neighbouring grids match). `M3D("GROUND",x,y)` is the height of the function (0 when none is defined) *without* any object: the flat boxes are in it, trees and walls are not. `M3D CLEAR` and `M3D RESET` forget the function. A grid is a plain mesh: walkable (`M3D("FLOORAT")` follows its triangles, which are the function only at the grid points), solid, drawn by both renderers, kept by the hardware renderer's buffers. Tests: `m3dtest1` (ground values, counts, floor, pictures seen from above, errors), `contest1`. In `demos/1987.bas` a chunk of 9 x 9 cells is one `M3D TERRAIN` call (before: 100 `M3D VERTEX` and 81 `M3D QUAD` calls with 100 sine sums in BASIC; 130 chunks took 40 ticks, now 20).
- **Mountains and lakes in the ground function: `M3D MOUNTAINS`, `M3D LAKES`, `M3D GROUNDQUIET`** (2026-11-20, roadmap item 95). `M3D MOUNTAINS seed,scale,amp,octaves,lo,hi` adds `amp * t * RIDGED(seed, x / scale, y / scale, octaves)` to the height, where `t` is a smoothstep of a slow noise (`FBM` of 2 octaves at four times the scale, -1 .. 1) between `lo` and `hi` (-1 <= lo < hi <= 1): ridged noise has sharp crests, so ranges and peaks appear only where the slow noise is high (lo 0.05, hi 0.4 covers about 8 % of the plane with ground above 120); `amp` 0 removes the layer. `M3D LAKES seed,scale,depth,lo,hi` lowers the ground by `depth * t` where an `FBM` of 2 octaves at `scale` is between `lo` and `hi` (basins that the game fills with water); `depth` 0 removes it. `M3D GROUNDQUIET cx,cy,rx,ry,margin` keeps both out of a box and fades them in over `margin` (the hand-made area of a game); margin 0 removes it. The order in `m3d_ground()`: base + hills, + mountains, - lakes, then the flat boxes multiply. All of them reset with `M3D CLEAR`; `M3D TERRAIN` and `M3D("GROUND")` see them. `RIDGED(seed,x,y,octaves[,gain[,lacunarity]])` is the language function behind it (`docs/noise.md`). Tests: `m3dtest1` (the formula, removal, quiet box, errors), `noisetest1` / `vmtest1` (RIDGED golden values from the Python port).
- **Instances: `M3D INSTANCE`, `M3D OBJSCALE`, `M3D OBJFREE`** (2026-11-20, roadmap item 95): many objects of one shape without a copy of the mesh. `M3D INSTANCE o,src[,x,y,z[,yaw[,scale]]]` makes object `o` (made again if it exists) **share the mesh of object `src`** (it must be defined; not itself), with its own place, heading and size (`scale` > 0, default 1: vertices and heights are multiplied, the picture coordinates are not). Flags and light are copied from `src` (change them afterwards with `OBJFLAGS` / `OBJLIGHT`). The sharing is **copy on write**: the vertices and triangles are reference counted (`obj_t.mref`), the last holder frees them, and an object that gets a vertex or a triangle added (`VERTEX`, `FACE`, `QUAD`, `BOX`, `TERRAIN`) while it shares gets a private copy first, so changing the prototype later does not change the instances and the other way round; making `src` again (`M3D OBJECT`) leaves the instances as they were. An instance of an instance works. The usual way: build the prototype, hide it (`M3D OBJSHOW p,0`: a hidden object is neither drawn nor solid), place the instances. `M3D OBJSCALE o,scale` changes the size of an object later. **`M3D OBJFREE a,b`** deletes the objects a .. b (undefined afterwards, the memory is freed; `M3D("OBJON",n)` is 0; unused numbers are fine). The software renderer works on the instance's own world coordinates (so scale costs nothing extra); the hardware renderer keeps one vertex buffer per object and gets the size as a uniform (`u_scale`), so a hardware instance still has its own buffer (true GPU instancing, one buffer for all, is the next step if the meshes ever show up in a measurement). Tests: `m3dtest1` (shape, size, turn, copy on write both ways, an instance of an instance, object made again, a picture of an instance, `OBJFREE`, errors, 300 random instances and changes; also clean under ASan / UBSan). In `demos/1987.bas` the three tree kinds are built once (objects 340 .. 342, `TREEPROTO`) and every tree is an instance: building 130 chunks takes 13 ticks instead of 20.

## Renderer interface

The module is split into the world model (`m3d.c`: objects, things, lamps, camera, collision, rays, the operations and queries) and a renderer behind the interface `m3drend_t` in `m3dpriv.h` (`create`, `destroy`, `resize`, `palette`, `render`). The model owns the palette and everything that behavior depends on, so a query answers the same whatever draws. The software rasterizer is `m3dsoft.c` (`m3d_soft_renderer`; its color map, translucency table, bitmap and depth buffer are its own). A second backend (OpenGL 2, 32 bit ARGB output) is planned; see `SESSION.md`.

### Choosing the renderer (`M3D SOFTWARE`, `M3D HARDWARE`)

`M3D SOFTWARE` switches to the software renderer (always possible, the default). `M3D HARDWARE` switches to the OpenGL 2 renderer; where there is none it is the error "Hardware renderer not available". `M3D("RENDERER")` is 0 or 1 for the one in use, `M3D("HWAVAIL")` is 1 when `M3D HARDWARE` can succeed (a program asks before it switches). `M3D RESET` keeps the choice; the palette and the view carry over a switch. The model (`m3d.c`) does not know OpenGL: the window host registers the hardware renderer with `m3d_set_hardware()` (`m3dgl.[ch]`: `m3d_gl_renderer()`); the console host registers none. Switching builds the new renderer's state first, so nothing changes when that fails. `m3dtest1` tests the switching with a stand-in renderer.

**Status: the renderer exists** (`m3dgl.c`, step 4); the choice in the 3D preferences and the default (hardware when `M3D("HWAVAIL")`) exist since step 5: `W3K_MESH` in `w3dkit.lib` selects the hardware renderer when it is available, `prefs3d.lib` has a **Renderer** row (Software / Hardware, saved as the sixth line of `local:3dprefs.cfg`, shown only for programs that use `W3K_MESH` when hardware is available).

### The OpenGL binding (`glload.[ch]`)

No GL library is linked. SDL opens the system's library (`SDL_GL_LoadLibrary`) and hands out addresses (`SDL_GL_GetProcAddress`); `glload_bind()` keeps the 60 functions the renderer needs (GL 1.1 drawing and textures, buffers, GLSL shaders, framebuffer objects under the core, ARB or EXT names) in a `glapi_t` while a context is current; one missing function means "no OpenGL 2". `glload_probe()` runs once in the screen thread when the window opens: it makes a hidden window and context, binds, reads `GL_VERSION` (>= 2.0), destroys them again and gives the current context back to the SDL renderer; the result is an atomic state (`glload_state()`, `glload_info()` with the version, the renderer's name and the reason for a "no"). `ENGINE1999_NOGL=1` skips the probe (answer: no). `gltest1` (in `make check`; skipped without a display or without OpenGL 2, `GLTEST_REQUIRE=1` makes a skip a failure) checks the probe, the binding and real drawing: a shader fills a framebuffer object and `glReadPixels` returns it (seen with an NVIDIA driver and with Mesa's llvmpipe under Xvfb).

### Pictures: palette bitmap or ARGB

A renderer says in `m3drend_t.argb` what its picture is. The software renderer makes a palette bitmap (`m3d_render()`, copied into the target layer by `sdlscr_gfx_putbuf()`); a hardware renderer makes 32 bit ARGB (`m3d_render32()`; `m3d_render()` returns 0 for it and the other way round, `m3d_is_argb()` tells which) and the window host puts it into the 32 bit layer, `sdlscr_3d_put()` (`docs/graphics-subsystem.md`). The colormap and the translucency table are software only; a hardware renderer lights and blends in true color.

### The hardware renderer (`m3dgl.c`)

**Where it runs:** on the thread that calls `M3D RENDER` (the program thread), with an OpenGL context of its own on a hidden window (made at `M3D HARDWARE`, deleted when the renderer is left or the module freed); the context is made current again at each frame if another thread had it. The screen thread, its SDL renderer and the probe are not involved, there is no hand-over of a context between threads. If the program thread ever changed while the context is current on the old one, switching would fail (not seen: the VM runs on one thread). The picture goes into the 32 bit layer by the host, `sdlscr_3d_put()`.

**What it draws, in this order** (as the software renderer): the sky (a fullscreen triangle whose shader looks the picture up per pixel from the view direction exactly as `draw_sky_bg()` does, or a flat clear color), the opaque objects (**kept meshes since 2026-11-15**: every object's triangles sit in a vertex buffer of their own in the object's own coordinates, grouped by picture; per frame the CPU only tests the object's bounding sphere and draws it with its place, heading and light as uniforms, one draw call per picture; back faces are culled by the hardware (`GL_CULL_FACE`, front faces wind clockwise because the picture is rendered upside down) for objects that are not two sided; the vertex shader moves the object into the world, makes the view and the projection with the same pinhole camera, the near plane 2 and a far plane from `M3D FAR`; the picture is rendered upside down so that the first row read back is the top one), the things (a rectangle facing the viewer at the depth of the thing's middle, back to front, no depth write), the translucent objects (back to front, mixed half and half). **Pictures** are uploaded once as 8 bit index textures (`GL_LUMINANCE`, nearest, repeating; again when `tex_t.gen` changed), the palette is a 256 x 1 texture and the fragment shader looks the index up in it (index 0 of a masked picture is discarded); a flat-color face uses an identity texture. **Light:** the object's light plus the lamps' light at the point (`amount * (1 - distance / radius)`), times the fog (`1 - depth / fog`), as in the software renderer, but as a smooth factor on the true color instead of 32 steps and the nearest palette color. **Palette:** the host hands the target layer's palette over before each frame (`M3D COLORMAP` is not needed in hardware mode; a palette fade shows at once).

**Tests:** `m3dgltest1` (in `make check`, skipped without display or OpenGL 2) draws ten scenes with both renderers (plain, light, fog, lamp, flat and picture sky, masked, translucent, things, camera) and compares them pixel by pixel on a ramp palette: at most a few percent of the pixels may differ (the measured mean difference is below 1.2 of 255; the differences are edge pixels); it also checks that a changed view and a changed picture reach the hardware renderer. `m3dglhost.sh` (in `make hosttest`) runs the real window host under Xvfb: a typed-in program draws a scene with the software renderer, switches with `M3D HARDWARE`, draws again and switches back, and the screenshots are compared (the two pictures were identical byte for byte).

**Speed (measured 2026-11-14 under Xvfb, where OpenGL is Mesa's CPU renderer llvmpipe, so the hardware numbers say little about a real GPU):** Deqq's House II at 640 x 264, 120 frames turning in place at four places: software 2.4 .. 4.3 ms per frame (150 .. 2260 triangles drawn), hardware 4.3 .. 13 ms (320 .. 3240 submitted). **On the owner's machine (NVIDIA GT 710, 2026-11-14) the hardware renderer is faster:** software 2.2 .. 4.3 ms per frame, hardware 1.7 .. 2.1 ms (about 2 times, and nearly independent of the scene: the readback and upload dominate, not the triangles). **Scale 1 on the owner's GT 710 (2026-11-14):** hardware 2.2 .. 2.9 ms per frame at 640 x 264 (1.8 .. 1.9 ms at 320 x 132), software 7.2 .. 14.3 ms (2.2 .. 4.2 ms at 320 x 132): at the full size hardware is 3 to 5 times faster, and software at 14 ms is near the 16.7 ms frame budget. **Scale 1 (640 x 264 instead of 320 x 132), Xvfb / llvmpipe:** software 7.1 .. 14 ms per frame (3.5 times slower), hardware 8.3 .. 10 ms (CPU-emulated GL, so not meaningful); the real GPU's numbers: see `m3dbench.sh` (it now measures both scales). **The 3D preferences have the render size option (320 x 132 or 640 x 264; default 640 x 264 with hardware).**

**`sh m3dbench.sh` repeats the measurement on any machine** (it opens the window; type `LOAD "demos/deqq2"` and `RUN`; the program `BENCH` in `deqq2.bas` runs when the environment variable `DEQQ2_BENCH` names an output file). Per frame the hardware path collects the triangles on the CPU, sorts them, uploads and reads the picture back (`glReadPixels`), so on a fast machine it is not automatically quicker than the software renderer; not measured on a real GPU.

**Kept meshes (2026-11-15, owner: "implement keeping the graphics buffers of unchanged objects, by implementing the changed-flag per object in m3d.c and kept buffers in m3dgl.c"):** `obj_t.gen` (m3dpriv.h) is the **generation of an object's mesh**: a new number from a global counter whenever a vertex or a triangle is added or the object is made again (`M3D OBJECT`); moving or turning (`M3D OBJPOS`), the light, the flags and showing / hiding leave it alone (the old `dirty` flag stays what it was: world coordinates and bounds are made again, for the software renderer, the collision and the shots). `m3d_t.presepoch` counts the times a picture number got or lost its picture (`M3D TEXTURE`, `M3D RESET`). In `m3dgl.c` every object has a `gobj_t` (`gl_t.gobjs`, one per object number, allocated at the first frame): its buffer, the `gen` and `presepoch` it was built for and the list of picture ranges; `draw_gobj()` builds the buffer (`build_gobj()`: texels in the buffer, the picture's size is the uniform `u_texsz`, so a picture that is replaced by one of another size needs no new mesh; flat colors use the identity picture) when `gen` or `presepoch` differs and draws it, so **an unchanged object costs no CPU work per frame but its bounding test and a few draw calls, and no upload**; the billboards (things) still go through the small stream buffer. `M3D("DRAWN")` is not counted while drawing any more (the hardware culls): `m3drend_t.drawn` counts it when asked, from the camera of the last frame and the objects as they are then (same numbers as the software renderer: the tests compare them). `m3d_gl_built()` tells how many meshes were built (for tests: `m3dgltest1` checks that a second frame, a moved, turned, dimmed object builds nothing, a changed object builds one, a new picture number builds all). **What it costs:** more draw calls (one per object and picture instead of one per picture: the Bug Hunt style worlds with some 700 objects in view give a thousand or so), a meshes' memory in the graphics card (about 20 bytes per vertex), and objects that change every frame (John's body, the drone beams) are built again every frame as before. **Not measured on a real GPU** (Xvfb / llvmpipe: Mesa's CPU renderer: 35 .. 38 ms a frame before and 34 ms after in 1987, which says only that nothing got worse there); the owner's GT 710 is the place to look (`DEQQ2_BENCH` / `m3dbench.sh`).

**Not done / differences:** no clipping of the object list by a spatial structure; kept meshes of objects that were cleared stay in the graphics memory until the object number is used again; things stay in the stream. The three differences of the first version are gone (2026-11-14): `M3D("DRAWN")` counts the same triangles as the software renderer (what is left after clipping at the near plane; things are not counted by either), index 0 of a plain picture and a missing sky (no color, no picture) are see-through (the picture's alpha is 0 there and `sdlscr_3d_put()` leaves such pixels transparent, so the layers below show, as with software; ARGB renderers must set the alpha: 0 = transparent, anything else = opaque). The light is smooth, so hardware pictures have no banding.

**Window glass, the translucent-object bug and palette alpha (2026-11-14, owner: the west front had a gap above the door and the windows were bare holes; then "use a tinted texture with an alpha channel (from the palette) for the glass, plain color").**
- **Bug (both renderers):** the list of translucent objects and the list of things shared the scratch arrays `m3d_t.order` / `odist`; the things were drawn *between* collecting and drawing the translucent objects, so with any thing in the world the translucent objects were skipped or the wrong ones drawn (the 28 glass panes of Deqq's House II, which has 69 things, vanished). The things now use the upper part of the arrays (`M3D_MAXOBJECTS` on); `m3dtest1` has a case (it fails without the fix).
- **Palette alpha:** in a *translucent* object (flag 2) a texel whose palette entry has an alpha of its own (`GFX PALETTE n,i,RGB(r,g,b,a)`, a < 255) is mixed with that alpha (a = 0: nothing is drawn there); entries with alpha 255 mix half and half as before, opaque and masked objects ignore the palette alpha. Software: a table per alpha level (alpha / 8, 1 .. 31, `alpha_blend()` in `m3dsoft.c`, made when first needed, about 50 ms, thrown away with a palette change); hardware: the palette texture carries the alpha and the fragment shader takes it for translucent objects. `m3dtest1` (exact values 75 = 50 * 3/4 + 150 / 4, alpha 0) and `m3dgltest1` ("palette alpha", both renderers compared). A picture number with no picture draws the palette color of that number flat, so **a plain tinted glass needs no picture** (give the panes an unused picture number and define that palette entry with an alpha). The glass of Deqq's House II does *not* use this: its panes (objects 1058 .. 1085, flags 10, thin boxes in the window holes, one on each side of the wall) use picture number 11, which is the **sky picture**, so the glass shows clouds (an accident of the numbering that the owner liked; `docs/deqq2.md` §4.4). The palette alpha is tested but no game uses it yet.
- The wall above the west kitchen door (x 172 .. 180, y 520 .. 584, z 100 .. 300) was missing from object 1 and is closed.

**Cone lamps (2026-11-14, owner: street lamps that shine down at an angle).** `M3D LAMP n,x,y,z,radius,amount[,dx,dy,dz,angle]`: with a direction (any length but not 0) and a half angle (1 .. 179 degrees) the lamp is a cone: the light at a point is `amount * (1 - distance / radius) * spot`, `spot = (cos(angle to the axis) - cos(half angle)) / (1 - cos(half angle))` clamped to 0 .. 1, so it is full on the axis and zero at the edge of the cone; without direction and angle the lamp shines all round as before (one lamp keeps one mode: setting it again replaces it). Software: the per-pixel lamp loop of `m3dsoft.c`; hardware: a second uniform array `u_lampd` (xyz = the axis, w = the cosine, -2 = no cone) in the fragment shader of `m3dgl.c`; `lamp_t` got `dx, dy, dz, cc, spot`. Tests: `m3dtest1` (on the axis lit, 76 degrees off the axis dark, aimed away dark, the error cases), `m3dgltest1` ("spot lamp", both renderers compared). **A fix that came with it:** `GFX_MAXARGS` was 8 although `M3D BOX` has 9 arguments, so the VM wrote one `double` past its argument array at every `M3D BOX`; it is 12 now. Cost and use: `docs/deqq2.md` §11.1 (the game gives its spare lamp slots to the nearest street lamps every 0.1 s; software rendering: a lamp costs about 2 ms a frame at 640 x 264 in a street, 0.5 ms at 320 x 132; each triangle only carries the lamps whose reach touches its bounding box, `tri_t.lampmask`; outside a cone is found without a square root).
